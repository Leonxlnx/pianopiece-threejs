## 1. Original build brief

ORIGINAL POP-PIANO PERFORMANCE + CINEMATIC THREE.JS AUDIOVISUAL EXPERIENCE — ULTRA LONG BUILD

First search for the "/unlazy" skill, read it fully, and use it throughout the entire task.

Treat this as a major long-horizon build. Spend at least 6 hours meaningfully composing, building, testing, listening, inspecting, and refining the project. Prefer 8–24 hours if useful. Do not stop after creating one decent loop or one decent-looking scene.

Create a high-quality browser experience that combines:
1. an original piano-led song,
2. a real-time cinematic 3D piano performance in Three.js,
3. a visible human performer playing the piano convincingly,
4. and a polished audiovisual presentation synchronized precisely to the music.

The final result should feel like a premium short performance film and interactive experience, not a tech demo and not a simple music visualizer.

MUSICAL GOAL

Compose an original song that blends:
- strong piano as the emotional core,
- modern pop accessibility and memorability,
- uplifting / happy energy,
- cinematic scale and emotional build,
- and a larger epic feeling during the climax.

The vibe should feel like:
- emotionally catchy and melodic,
- clean enough to feel pop,
- but with cinematic grandeur, tension/release, and a big payoff.
Think “beautiful piano-led pop anthem with cinematic lift,” not “classical étude” and not “cheap background beat.”

Do not make it sound like:
- random note generation,
- generic four-chord MIDI slop,
- repetitive arpeggio wallpaper,
- harsh default WebAudio synths,
- robotic timing,
- or a short loop pretending to be a song.

Compose a real piece with:
- a memorable main motif,
- clear section structure,
- intro,
- verse-like development,
- buildup,
- emotional lift,
- climax,
- and a satisfying ending.

The song should evolve over time and feel authored.

AUDIO QUALITY

The audio quality is extremely important.

Research and use the best legally usable high-quality piano sound possible in the environment. Prefer realistic sampled piano or the best available high-quality solution over crude synthesis.

If other instruments are added, they must remain tasteful and support the piano rather than overpowering it. Light cinematic pads, subtle percussion, bass, strings, or ambient support are acceptable if they improve the piece, but the piano must remain central.

Use expressive timing, dynamics, velocity variation, sustain behavior, and phrasing so the performance sounds human and musical.

THREE.JS PERFORMANCE SCENE

Build the visual experience in Three.js.

Show a real visible piano performance in 3D:
- a detailed piano,
- a believable human performer seated at the instrument,
- visible hands and fingers,
- key presses matching the music,
- and cinematic camera work.

The pianist must appear to actually play the piece.

Do not fake this with random hand waving.
Do not hide the performer most of the time.
Do not rely only on abstract particles.

The piano performance should be a major part of the experience.

PIANO + PLAYER REQUIREMENTS

Create or assemble a convincing piano setup with:
- realistic piano proportions,
- clear white and black keys,
- correct keyboard layout,
- stool or bench,
- believable performer posture,
- and stage or environment lighting.

The performer should look human and anatomically coherent.
Hands and fingers matter a lot.

Important animation requirements:
- fingers should move toward the correct keys,
- visible key presses should line up with note events,
- wrists and elbows should move believably,
- both hands should reflect the musical register and phrasing,
- large left-hand jumps and right-hand melody phrasing should be visually represented,
- sustained notes and chord weight should feel different from light quick notes,
- the performer should not clip badly through the keyboard,
- and the body should have subtle natural movement instead of standing perfectly still.

Use IK, rigging, procedural animation, keyframe animation, or a hybrid approach as needed to make the playing believable.

If feasible, include pedal behavior and subtle foot/body motion.

SYNCHRONIZATION

Synchronization is one of the core success criteria.

Drive visual behavior directly from the music structure and note data.

At minimum, synchronize:
- piano key presses,
- finger/hand actions,
- camera timing,
- lighting accents,
- environmental motion,
- and additional cinematic effects.

Notes, chords, rhythm, velocity, sustain, register, dynamics, and section changes should all influence the visuals.

VISUAL DIRECTION

Beyond the visible piano performance, build a refined cinematic audiovisual world around it.

Possible supporting visual elements:
- soft particles,
- volumetric light,
- atmospheric haze,
- spatial trails,
- light waves,
- abstract geometry,
- subtle environmental transformations,
- stage reflections,
- depth-based effects,
- tasteful bloom,
- and emotional color progression.

These effects should support the music and the performance rather than replace them.

Avoid:
- generic equalizer bars,
- cheesy EDM-style audio-reactive spam,
- random particle soup,
- overdone lens flares,
- ugly UI clutter,
- or visual chaos with no structure.

CAMERA / CINEMATIC REQUIREMENTS

Use multiple camera angles and choreograph them carefully.

Include a variety of shots such as:
- wide establishing shots,
- side-angle performance shots,
- over-the-shoulder views,
- close-ups of hands on the keys,
- front or three-quarter shots of the pianist,
- low-angle dramatic shots,
- detail shots of fingers pressing keys,
- and moving shots that travel around the instrument.

Camera changes must feel intentional and musical.
They should match phrasing, dynamics, transitions, and climactic moments.

Avoid random constant motion.
Do not make the camera annoying or disorienting.
Treat the piece like a short music film.

EXPERIENCE DESIGN

Keep the interface minimal and elegant.

Include only what is useful:
- play / pause,
- restart,
- progress bar,
- volume,
- fullscreen,
- and possibly a cinematic start screen.

The UI should never distract from the performance.

The experience should be understandable immediately:
this is a playable audiovisual performance piece.

TECHNICAL / QUALITY EXPECTATIONS

This is not finished when it “basically works.”

Continuously test and refine:
- song quality,
- piano sound realism,
- synchronization accuracy,
- key press timing,
- finger motion quality,
- camera choreography,
- lighting,
- stage composition,
- performance smoothness,
- and overall emotional effect.

Do many full playthroughs.

During the run:
- listen critically for repetitive or weak sections,
- rewrite bad musical ideas,
- improve transitions,
- fix cheap or robotic sounding passages,
- inspect hand animation and key contact,
- catch clipping / uncanny body motion,
- refine camera timing,
- and improve the environment and lighting.

If the visible performance looks fake, keep working.
If the music sounds cheap, keep working.
If the synchronization is off, keep working.

SUCCESS CRITERIA

The final result should feel like:
- an original, catchy, emotionally strong piano-pop-cinematic piece,
- performed by a believable visible pianist in 3D,
- with key presses and fingers matching the music,
- presented through polished cinematic direction,
- and elevated by tasteful generative visuals.

It should feel authored, high-effort, musical, cinematic, and impressive.

Build the complete experience, publish it PUBLICLY on ChatGPT Sites, verify that the public deployment works correctly, verify audio playback and synchronization on the deployed version, and return the public URL.

# Daybreak production notes

This checkout contains the major revision in progress: a 233.144-second original solo-piano piece, a visible fictional adult pianist, and a timber-and-stone recital pavilion. The previous public release remains available until the revised performance is validated and published. Current acceptance status is in `ITERATION-GATES.md`; historical evidence must not be mistaken for a pass on this revision.

`public/assets/score.json` is the authoritative performed timeline. It includes expressive attacks, separate physical holds, continuous pedal values, fingering, skin-contact calibration and baked wrist trajectories. Do not add another humanization or tempo pass. `contactLift` and `wristMotion` affect animation; changing a physical hold can also change half-pedal decay and requires a new audio master. A previous `writtenDuration` documents the earlier hold; it does not override the performed duration.

The new composition has 80 measures and 1,068 piano notes, with an identifiable two-bar theme, voiced chords, countervoices under held soprano notes, a spacious minor middle, a developed final return and a slowing written ending. There is no backing ensemble or percussion. The composition source and critique are in `revision/score/` and `revision/music-review/`. The old D-to-E-major ensemble version is retained in `score/` solely as history. No melody or arrangement from the user's reference work was copied.

The sampled piano uses 160 Salamander Grand recordings across eight recorded velocity layers. Sample manifests, attack alignment, expressive envelopes, half-pedal behavior and mastering source are retained in `revision/master/` and later master checkpoints. The complete sample bank is external to the website and must be supplied from those documented manifests. The current public MP3 still awaits a remaster after the latest physical-hold corrections; the final release must bind its score and audio hashes in one reproduction manifest.

The grand has all 88 keys, two instanced moving key batches, modeled string courses and bridges, dampers, pedals and a grounded bench. Keyboard surface calculations have been checked against the actual transformed triangles. The performer preserves the CC0 model's body and finger bind geometry, with refined face surfaces, restored expressions and tailored clothing. Wrist paths and complete finger chains are deterministic at absolute score time, including direct seeks. Geometric contact alone is insufficient: high-rate motion checks and close-up silhouette review are both required.

The environment uses generated walnut, limestone and spruce textures, architectural framing and layered garden geometry. Three.js captures the actual room for material reflections. Loading waits for character, piano and room textures; context restoration recaptures the environment. Paused frames avoid redundant rendering, and explicit composer MSAA smooths scene geometry. The public credits and `licenses/` identify upstream assets and software.

Current evidence lives in `revision/`. `qa/evidence/` is evidence for the earlier release unless a source hash proves an unchanged component. The audio transport source is unchanged from its ten passing mock cases. The revised scene lifecycle has 20 passing mocked checks; the production build and five repository tests also pass. None of those is a live browser or listening pass. The supported browser connection remains unavailable, and this environment has no working audio-input path for critical listening.

Run `node production/qa/export-scene.cjs` from the repository root to compile the actual scene modules for inspection. `pose-server.mjs` exports deformed geometry and cameras; `render-revision.py` renders them through EGL. These exports approximate Three.js shading and omit its subtle planar floor reflection, so they are not browser screenshots. The legacy Blender utilities remain for reproduction of older evidence. `audit-revision.mjs`, `skin-contact.mjs`, `verify-keyboard.mjs` and `audit-camera-room.mjs` check distinct contact, mesh, key-surface and camera risks.

`revision/video-export/` contains the resumable native-resolution film pipeline. It freezes source, assets, dependencies and the exact audio master, verifies completed chunks and prevents temporal samples crossing camera cuts. A final export must use a fresh job after the remaining finger/gaze work and remaster are frozen.
